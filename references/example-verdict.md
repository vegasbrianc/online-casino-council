# Example verdict — fictional operator

For shape and tone only. **Aurora Casino** does not exist; the market, regulator and figures are
invented. Use this to see what a finished verdict looks like, not as a source for any number.

**Context file summary.** Aurora is licensed in a regulated European market by a national
regulator, casino and live casino only, six licensed competitors, offshore blocked. NGR
€1.4M/month (measured), blended CPA €310 (measured), 12-month NGR per FTD €520 (estimated),
bonus cost 31% of GGR (measured). Head of Online owns the marketing budget; new markets need
board approval. Red line: no crypto payments.

**The decision put to the council.** Launch a "€500 welcome package" (100% match up to €500 plus
200 free spins) to replace the current €100 offer, aiming to lift FTDs by 40%. Leaning yes.

---

## Online Casino Council Verdict

**In one line:** Don't ship the €500 package; ship a €150 offer with a 20x wagering cap and a
90-day cohort test, because the plan lifts FTDs by buying players who never make a second deposit.

### The Question
Replace the €100 welcome offer with a €500 match plus 200 spins in a regulated market with six
licensed rivals, to raise FTDs 40%. Current bonus cost is already 31% of GGR.

### Where They Agree
Six of ten oppose it outright, and none of the other four defend it as written. The offer optimises the metric that is easiest to move and hardest to bank. FTDs
will rise. Second deposits will not, and the CFO, Retention and Payments seats each reached
"bonus cost above 40% of GGR" from different directions.

### Where They Clash
**The Director versus Compliance.** The Director wants to go bigger on a single channel for six
weeks and accept the abuse as the price of speed. Compliance says the creative as drafted cannot
run in this market at all, and affordability checks must sit before the bonus is credited.
Compliance wins on the creative and the checks; the Director wins on concentrating whatever
offer survives into one channel rather than spreading it.

**The Director versus Product.** The Director wants to buy the FTDs now. Product says the €500
is a patch for a funnel that is leaking: reg-to-FTD sits at 31%, nine points below where this
operator's own redesign put it eighteen months ago, and recovering those nine points delivers
more FTDs than the offer buys, at no bonus cost. The Director's answer is that funnel work takes
two quarters and the competitor is spending this one. Both are right on their own timescale,
which is why the recommendation runs the offer test and the funnel work in parallel instead of
picking one.

**Compliance versus the Gaming Board.** Compliance treats the whole package as unrunnable. The
Board disagrees on scope: it does not regulate the size of a welcome offer, and said so plainly,
so "we cannot do €500" is not its objection. What it would act on is narrower and harder — the
headline value shown without the wagering condition, and the absence of an affordability check
before credit. That distinction is worth money: the offer is not dead, the *creative and the
flow* are. Compliance was directionally right and specifically wrong, which is the usual shape of
this clash.

**The target itself.** The External Consultant says the board asked for FTDs because FTDs are easy to
count. The real target is 90-day NGR per acquired player, and by that measure the current €100
offer may already be the better one. Nobody disagreed; nobody had the number.

### Regulatory and Player-Harm Exposure
Touches the misleading-advertising rule (headline value versus wagering conditions), bonus T&C
transparency requirements, and affordability obligations at deposit.

**Compliance's route to yes:** creative pre-cleared showing headline and wagering condition
together, affordability check before the bonus is credited, per-game contribution capped.
**The Gaming Board's ruling:** offer size is not a matter for it, and it declined to object to
€500 as a number; the creative as drafted is a matter for it, and it would open a file.

Worst realistic outcome: a formal warning and forced withdrawal of the campaign, with the
regulator's attention on the brand for the following year. The recommendation survives it only
with pre-cleared creative and checks before crediting.

### Numbers That Decide It
- 90-day NGR per FTD by offer cohort. **Not measured.** The whole decision.
- FTD-to-second-deposit rate at the current €100 offer. **Measured**, and nobody brought it.
- Bonus cost as % of GGR under the new offer. **Estimated** at 38–44% by three seats independently.

### Conviction-Weighted Recommendation
Do not launch the €500 package. Run a €150 offer, 20x wagering, per-game contribution capped,
affordability check before crediting, creative pre-cleared by Compliance, on one channel for 90
days against the current offer as control. Decide on 90-day NGR per FTD, not on FTD count. High
confidence across the CFO, Compliance, Retention and Payments seats, with the Gaming Board
clearing the offer size and objecting only to the creative and the flow; the Director's dissent
is on tempo, not direction.

In parallel, over the same 90 days, run the reg-to-FTD funnel work Product proposed. It is the
only lever on the table that adds FTDs without adding a cent of bonus cost, and if it recovers
even half the nine points it beats both offers.

### The Bet
$1,000 that the €500 package lifts FTDs by more than 30% and lowers 90-day NGR per FTD below the
current offer's.
Confidence: 80%.
The assumption that would change my mind: a measured FTD-to-second-deposit rate above 55% on the
current offer, which would mean the base is healthier than the council assumed and can absorb a
richer top of funnel.
